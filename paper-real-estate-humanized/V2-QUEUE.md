- **Figure 5 was correct**; it matches `results/*/results.txt` exactly. **Table 1 was wrong** — it
  reported 44.9% uninformed / 2.3% unmatched where the truth is 16.7% / 32.2%, i.e. the two rows
  were effectively inverted. It was hand-written in the `.tex` with no generator, which is how it
  drifted. Now corrected in both versions, with a note stating the sample.
- **The §2.3 footnote contradicted the code.** It claimed the (d=1, K=0) cell was "excluded from the
  estimation sample"; in fact `classify_listings(unverified_disclosers="honest")` folds it into
  truthful disclosers, and neither `run.py` nor either config ever passes that argument. Confirmed
  arithmetically: estimation sample 834,816 vs full 843,621 — had 271,436 listings been dropped, N
  would be ≈572,000. Footnote rewritten to describe the actual treatment, with the legal basis.
- **Author's interpretation confirmed empirically.** K=0 means "no post-July-2021 certificate in the
  public registry", not "no certificate exists". The registry was populated from the reform date
  onward while pre-reform certificates remained legally valid (*décret* n° 2020-1610 of 17 December
  2020: 2013–2017 vintage valid to 31 Dec 2022; 2018–June 2021 vintage to 31 Dec 2024). The unmatched
  share falls monotonically as the registry fills — apartments 62.5% (2021Q3) → 18.4% (2024Q4),
  houses 73.1% → 43.7%.
- **Direction of bias established.** A seller with a still-valid pre-reform certificate who withholds
  it is recorded as uninformed rather than as a hider, so σ̂₀ understates. Since e(σ₀) is decreasing
  in σ₀, ĉ₀ is overstated — the coverage gap reinforces the upper-bound reading rather than
  threatening it.
- **The 6.9 vs 7.7 puzzle is closed too.** 29,676/(29,676+401,986) = 6.9% is the *pooled* hiding rate
  among informed sellers; 7.7% is the *unweighted mean across the 40 markets*. Both correct, now
  labelled as such. Houses: 2.7% pooled vs 3.7% market-mean.
- **Figure 5's legend was misleading** — the 79.8% bar was labelled "(K=1, d=1)" but contains all
  d=1 including the 32.2% unmatched. Relabelled "Discloses a score (d=1)" and regenerated.

## Identification — substantive

4. **~~ρ̂ is attenuated because parallel trends do not establish ρ_pre ≈ 0.~~ Withdrawn — I was
   wrong.** §6.1 already handles this explicitly: *"The DiD does not require that buyers were
   entirely inattentive to EPC scores before July 2021; it requires only that their relative
   pricing of disclosure groups was not already trending."* It further argues the bias runs
   *upward* (both a supply-side shift and any opposability-induced change in c₀ would inflate Δ̂
   and hence ρ̂), which is why ρ̂ is presented as an upper bound. My attenuation claim contradicted
   that and does not survive.

   What remains, much smaller: the corrected §2.1 now states that apartments had a pre-reform
   *level* premium, and Figure 1 makes it visible. A reader may wonder whether that indicates
   ρ_pre > 0. One sentence in §3.3 or §4.4 attributing the level premium to composition — the
   concentration of non-audited stock in high-price urban markets, which is already the paper's
   explanation — would close the question. Worth adding; not a defect.

5. **Promote the stability-of-the-undisclosed-share identification argument.**
   The share of undisclosed listings is stable over time, especially for apartments (documented
   as a stylized fact in §2.2, "Non-Disclosure Persists Long After Mandatory Disclosure"). This
   is a genuinely clean identification argument and is currently under-used: a stable
   non-disclosure share implies the composition of the silent pool, and hence the equilibrium
   belief ê\*, does not shift across the reform. The jump in the premium therefore cannot be a
   supply-side compositional artefact and is attributable to demand-side repricing — ρ moving
   from near zero to positive. It should appear in §4.4 (identification), be previewed in the
   introduction, and be stated explicitly as the reason Δ̂ maps into ρ.
   *(Author's phrasing was "adjustment cannot come from demand but supply"; recorded here as
   supply being what stays fixed, licensing the demand-side attribution. Confirm.)*
