## What needs you (estimand, assumption or reporting language, none mechanical), ranked

1. **The sign of rho in prose** (P2 #1, confirmed independently by P5). Fewer energy-sensitive buyers make hiding LESS attractive in the model (d sigma0*/d rho > 0; the premium in Partial Pooling does not depend on rho). Section 2 L86-87 and main.tex Conclusion L343 say the opposite. The Conclusion sentence at L343 ("buyer heterogeneity makes exploiting that ambiguity financially worthwhile") is the one I rewrote on 2026-11-03 when replacing "None of the three suffices alone"; it reproduced the claim. The abstract is not affected ("buyers differ in how sensitive they are to energy efficiency"). The Section 2 sentence is yours; both need a rewrite in terms of the revenue channel.
2. **The listing-classification table in the appendix** (P7 #1, blocker, verified). It excludes as "Falsification" the 32.2% and 51.2% of disclosed-unmatched listings that Section 3 keeps as truthful disclosers. Delete it or replace it with Table tab:groups. Related: K is defined as true information status in the appendix (P7 #2) vs "no post-2021 registry record" in Section 3; theta is a grade there and binary quality in the model (P7 #3); rho is a share there and an unclipped effective-attention ratio in the estimates (P7 #4); the matching appendix the footnote promises does not exist (P7 #5).
3. **Robustness section built on the full-attention cost** (P6 F1-F3). The headline hiding cost is the limited-attention predicted premium (mean Delta-hat, 8.5 and 3.9, apartments above houses); the robustness table reports c0-hat^FA (2.41 and 3.45, reverse order) and draws conclusions about "the paper's hiding cost". Statements identify rho_m itself where only rho omega1 is identified; the "raw ratio is about 1 regardless" claim omits the hypotheses it needs and 89% of apartment markets have a ratio above 1.
4. **Estimation text vs body** (P3 #1-3): the appendix reads the bound violations as a second force, the body says not; linear pricing is needed for the psi inversion too (exact logit at psi .13 would give psi-hat .29); selection does not bias both estimators toward full attention (wrong for the regression through the origin; houses move away from 1 in simulation).
5. **Benchmark proofs** (P5 #1-5): prop:premium is false with hiding costs as stated; its proof uses a logit pricing model rather than the linear pricing the benchmark claims; "empirically dominant regime" contradicts the paper's own regime counts (0 of 45 and 11 of 97); the cross-market predictions and the "expanding band" do not follow; "transfer from buyers" and "pure rent" in the welfare paragraph are wrong in the benchmark. The Conclusion carries the same welfare wording at L347.
6. **Section 5 proofs** (P4 #1-2): the saving bound is on the average of seller-specific subsidies, while the text compares it with a one-subsidy-per-market bill (the market-uniform saving can be 6.6 times the bound in the reviewer's example); Step 3 of the pi -> 1 proof is false as written (the conclusion survives by the intuitive criterion).
