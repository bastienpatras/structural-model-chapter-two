The comparative statics separate three channels. The {{M006}}revenue channel{{M007}} ({{M001}}) works as in Section{{M008}}{{M009}}: a larger effective attention to displayed ratings raises the gain from concealment and the hiding rate. The {{M010}}belief channel{{M011}} ({{M002}}) keeps its sign but is scaled by {{M003}}, because a higher share of informed sellers makes silence more informative only to buyers who think about what silence means. The {{M012}}attention channel{{M013}} ({{M004}}, {{M005}}) is new: more attention to silence, or a less optimistic default, lowers the gain from hiding and the hiding rate. These are the margins on which a salience policy acts (Section{{M014}}{{M015}}). The last statement in part{{M016}}(i) is the formal reason the single-cost model becomes fragile in apartments: when silence is barely read, a small change in the hiding cost produces a large swing in the hiding rate, because nothing on the buyer side pushes back.
