The term follows the limited-attention literature, in which an agent who does not attend to an attribute replaces it with a default value rather than with an inference {{M006}}. The default is therefore not a belief about the dwelling's rating. It is what the buyer implicitly assumes when the question of energy never arises. Three benchmark values fix ideas. With {{M001}} no energy cost is priced at all, which does not mean the buyer believes the dwelling consumes nothing, only that energy plays no part in her valuation. With {{M002}} equal to the prior mean cost {{M003}}, the inattentive buyer behaves like one who assumes an average dwelling, which is cursedness by another name. With {{M004}} she happens to subtract exactly what an attentive buyer would infer, and attention becomes irrelevant. The same default applies to rated and silent listings, because a buyer who does not attend to energy, by definition, does not use what the listing shows about it. Which value is relevant is an empirical question: Proposition{{M007}}{{M008}}(iii) shows that the extension can explain the apartment wedge only if {{M005}}, and Proposition{{M009}}{{M010}}(iii) shows what the data can say about its level.
