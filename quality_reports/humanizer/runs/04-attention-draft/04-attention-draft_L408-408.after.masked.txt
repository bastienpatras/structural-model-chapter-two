The over-identifying wedge is the part of the reform's price effect that silence escaped. Had silent listings received the same increase in attention as rated ones, their price relative to E-to-G listings would have moved by {{M001}}, as Section{{M003}}{{M004}} predicts; they received less, and the shortfall, priced at the cost of the silent pool, is {{M002}}. A spillover of one means the reform made energy something buyers ask about whether or not a rating is shown; a spillover near zero means it made the rating something buyers read but not something they ask for. Part{{M005}}(iii) states what the design cannot do. The two gaps pin down one number per market, the perceived cost of silence, and low attention with a modest default fits them as well as slightly higher attention with an even more optimistic one. Normalising the default to zero delivers the largest attention compatible with the data.{{FN1}}
