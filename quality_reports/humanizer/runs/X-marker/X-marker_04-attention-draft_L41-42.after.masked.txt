These amendments share the premise that the buyer facing a silent listing still thinks about its energy performance but reasons about it imperfectly. A large body of evidence suggests that consumers often do not think about costs that are not put in front of them, and that when they do not, they act as if those costs were small rather than average. Buyers under-react to sales taxes that are not included in posted prices {{M002}}, to road tolls collected electronically {{M003}} and to shrouded shipping fees {{M004}}. Energy operating costs are a documented instance in durable-goods markets {{M005}}. Models of focusing, salience and sparsity formalise the same idea, that the attributes a choice set makes salient receive more weight than those it leaves in the background {{M006}}. In financial markets, limited attention changes how disclosed and undisclosed information is priced {{M007}}.
