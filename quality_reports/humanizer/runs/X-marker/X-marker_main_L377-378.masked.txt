The broader lesson extends beyond energy labels. Disclosure mandates discipline strategic silence only when sellers are actually informed. When a substantial share genuinely does not know their product's quality, the mandate produces ambiguity rather than transparency. Involuntary non-disclosers shelter strategic hiders, and silence cannot be read as bad news. Where buyers do not attend to what a listing leaves out, silence is not read at all, and making absence visible is a complement to the other instruments. {{M001}}
 The policy challenge is not to punish opacity more severely but to produce knowledge in the first place. In housing energy markets, as in any market where non-disclosure mixes strategy with ignorance, audit-infrastructure investment is the instrument that expands the reach of every other tool in the regulator's toolkit.
