These two forces generate three equilibrium regimes. In the partial-pooling equilibrium that describes the bulk of French housing markets, the fraction of informed low-quality sellers that hides adjusts endogenously until the marginal hider is exactly indifferent between concealment and disclosure. This indifference condition yields the paper's central theoretical result. The non-disclosure premium equals exactly the concealment cost in partial pooling, invariant to the share of informed sellers and the share of energy-sensitive buyers. We identify the concealment cost directly from the disclosed-price gap and the registry-measured belief, without needing buyer energy-sensitivity to be separately estimated, and test the indifference prediction against it rather than assume it. The two segments concur less than an earlier reading of these facts suggested. Concealment costs are 2.4{{M002}} of property value for apartments and 3.5{{M003}} for houses, of the same order across two segments that differ in buyer energy-sensitivity (49.5{{M004}} for apartments, 45.8{{M005}} for houses, identified from an external energy-cost anchor rather than from the price data used to construct the premium itself); but the indifference prediction itself holds for houses and is decisively rejected for apartments, where the non-disclosure premium exceeds what the concealment cost and the registry-measured belief predict by a wide and precisely estimated margin. One attention parameter per segment, measuring how much buyers attend to energy on listings that show no rating, accounts for both the rejection in apartments and the fit in houses (Section{{M006}}{{M007}}). {{M001}}
Strategic hiding is itself uncommon. 15.2{{M008}} of informed apartment sellers with unfavorable ratings withhold them, versus 6.8{{M009}} for houses. The non-disclosure premium survives not because strategic hiding is pervasive but because genuine uninformedness is. The uninformed seller, who pays no concealment cost yet receives the same premium as the strategic hider, is the residual claimant on the information rent; the informed hider, in the segment where the model fits, merely breaks even.
