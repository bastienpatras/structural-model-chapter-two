The regulator has other instruments besides the subsidy, and they act on different margins of the seller's problem (Table{{M004}}{{M005}}). Enforcement against hiding raises the cost of concealment and changes who hides. Raising the share of informed sellers {{M002}} changes how many sellers can renovate at all. Two further instruments act on buyers: making silence as salient as a rating raises the attention buyers pay to a listing that shows no rating, and full attention raises the attention they pay to every listing. Three questions follow. Does each instrument move the renovation threshold {{M003}}, and with it the subsidy a seller needs? Does it change who hides? And would a simple measure such as making silence salient reduce what the state has to spend on subsidies? The next proposition answers the first two questions for each instrument, with the hiding cost held at its calibrated value, and the closing subsection returns to the third.
