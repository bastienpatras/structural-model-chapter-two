Raising $\sigma_0$ adds more low-quality hiders to the denominator while leaving the numerator unchanged, so $e$ falls. Raising $\sigma_1$ instead adds the same term $\pi\bar{\theta}$ to both numerator and denominator, and because $e < 1$ the numerator effect dominates, so $e$ rises. This strict decrease in $e$ as $\sigma_0$ rises is what makes the equilibrium in Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium} unique. As more informed low-quality sellers choose to hide, buyer beliefs worsen, which attenuates the benefit of hiding and restores incentive compatibility.
