Classical unraveling further predicts that, once disclosure is mandatory and verifiable, the share of non-disclosing listings should converge rapidly toward zero. The French data reject this prediction in both segments, though with different magnitudes. For houses (panel{{M010}}(b)), the corresponding numbers are {{M004}} pre-reform, {{M005}} post-reform, and a final-twelve-month mean of {{M006}}. Houses thus display better baseline audit coverage and faster post-reform convergence, but in both segments the share remains structurally bounded away from zero more than three years after disclosure became legally required. Almost three-tenths of apartment listings and one-tenth of house listings still come to market without a certified audit, indicating that a substantial fraction of sellers cannot disclose, because they do not possess a certified audit at the time of listing.
