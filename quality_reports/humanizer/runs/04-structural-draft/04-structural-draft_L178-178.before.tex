The numerator counts the high-quality mass in the pool, combining informed high-quality sellers who choose to hide ($\pi\bar{\theta}\sigma_1$) with uninformed sellers who happen to have high quality ($(1-\pi)\bar{\theta}$). The denominator is the total non-disclosing mass, so the belief $e$ is a weighted average across two economically distinct populations. When uninformed sellers dominate the pool, because $\pi$ or $\sigma_0$ is small, non-disclosure looks like a constraint rather than a choice and beliefs remain favorable to the seller. If instead strategic low-quality hiders make up most of the pool, with both $\pi$ and $\sigma_0$ large, the pool is contaminated and beliefs deteriorate accordingly. Unraveling is therefore incomplete, because non-disclosure is inherently ambiguous whenever $\pi < 1$, whereas in classical disclosure theory all sellers are informed and silence is unambiguous bad news.
