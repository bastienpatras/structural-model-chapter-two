Proposition{{M007}}{{M008}} is the familiar adverse-selection asymmetry, worked out for this setting. A high-quality seller has nothing to gain from ambiguity. Disclosure reveals her type exactly; non-disclosure pools her with low-quality hiders, and since {{M001}} for any non-degenerate pool, that comparison always binds. A low-quality seller faces the opposite incentive: concealment lets her price off {{M002}}, which is strictly above her true quality of zero whenever any uninformed sellers are present, so the gain from pooling is strictly positive. The hiding cost {{M003}} that falls only on high-quality concealers reinforces this separation but is not what drives it under the benchmark {{M004}}: pool inference alone is enough to make high-quality sellers disclose, and {{M005}} only starts to matter once {{M006}}, the distrust-discount case.
