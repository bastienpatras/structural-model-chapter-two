The partition of buyers into EPC-sensitive and EPC-insensitive types reflects a well-documented empirical regularity, since willingness to pay for energy efficiency varies sharply with how much of the ongoing energy cost the buyer will actually bear. Primary-residence buyers in northern France with long heating seasons, and investor-buyers who face the impending F/G rental ban under the {{M002}}loi Climat et R{{M003}}silience{{M004}}, have strong incentives to incorporate EPC scores into their bids. By contrast, a vacation-home buyer who occupies the property only a few weeks a year, or a buyer shopping in a mild climate, has much less riding on it and may rationally discount energy performance. This heterogeneity determines {{M001}}, the share of the local buyer pool that is EPC-sensitive.
