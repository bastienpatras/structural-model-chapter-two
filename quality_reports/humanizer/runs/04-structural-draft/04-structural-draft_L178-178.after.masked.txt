The numerator counts the high-quality mass in the pool, combining informed high-quality sellers who choose to hide ({{M001}}) with uninformed sellers who happen to have high quality ({{M002}}). The denominator is the total non-disclosing mass, so the belief {{M003}} is a weighted average across two economically distinct populations. When uninformed sellers dominate the pool, because {{M004}} or {{M005}} is small, non-disclosure looks like a constraint rather than a choice and beliefs remain favorable to the seller. If instead strategic low-quality hiders make up most of the pool, with both {{M006}} and {{M007}} large, the pool is contaminated and beliefs deteriorate accordingly. Unraveling is therefore incomplete, because non-disclosure is inherently ambiguous whenever {{M008}}, whereas in classical disclosure theory all sellers are informed and silence is unambiguous bad news.
