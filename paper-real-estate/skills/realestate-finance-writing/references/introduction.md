# The Introduction Funnel

Reconstructed paragraph-by-paragraph from the corpus. Roughly 12–14 paragraphs for a
full-length paper. Each paragraph has one job. The order is load-bearing: it moves from a
fact in the world, to a theory that fails to explain it, to a setting that can adjudicate,
to evidence, to a model that interprets, to contribution.

## ¶1 — The puzzle, then the theoretical benchmark

Three moves in one paragraph, tight:

1. An institutional fact stated flatly. *"Corporate disclosure mandates are pervasive."*
2. The tension. *"Yet, the need for these mandates is often challenged."*
3. The theoretical benchmark that creates the tension, with its canonical citations — then
   why its empirical status is unresolved (strong assumptions; hard to test).

No "in this paper we." No contribution claim. The reader should finish ¶1 knowing what
question the field has failed to settle, not what you did.

## ¶2 — "To learn about X, we examine Y"

Introduce the setting, with enough institutional texture that the reader understands the
setting is *chosen*, not merely available. The corpus spends real words here on
institutional mechanics — who publishes what, to whom, under what obligation.

## ¶3 — What the theory predicts *in this setting*, and why the setting can adjudicate

Restate the prediction in the vocabulary of your setting, then argue the setting is a
suitable testbed. This paragraph is where the phrase "testbed" or "laboratory" earns its
place.

## ¶4 — The assumption audit ★

The highest-value paragraph in the corpus, and the most commonly missing one.

Enumerate the assumptions the theoretical prediction rests on — literally *(a)*, *(b)*,
*(c)*, *(d)* — then walk through each and argue, from institutional detail, which ones your
setting plausibly satisfies. **Support the claims with evidence**, not assertion: the corpus
backs an assumed-credible-disclosure claim by showing the disclosed variable explains 58% of
the cross-section of valuations.

The purpose is to isolate the *one* assumption genuinely in question. That isolated
assumption becomes the paper's contribution.

## ¶5 — Name the assumption at stake

State that the prediction's descriptiveness now hinges on the single assumption you have
isolated, and that this assumption has received little attention relative to the others
(with citations showing the others *have* been studied). This converts "we test a theory"
into "we test the specific under-examined joint that the theory hangs on."

## ¶6 — Data

Sources, construction method, and what the data structure *enables*. Be concrete about
provenance and about effortful construction — hand-collection, OCR, record linkage. Effort
is signalled, not hidden. Close on the analytical affordance: *"the time-series dimension of
our data allows us to investigate…"*

## ¶7 — The descriptive findings, enumerated

*"The data reveal three striking descriptive findings. First… Second… Third…"*

Reduced-form facts, numbered, before any model. Then a one-sentence verdict against the
theory — including partial verdicts, which are more credible than clean rejections:
*"inconsistent with immediate unravelling, but suggest the prediction may be descriptive in
the medium to long run."*

## ¶8 — Model motivation

The pivot. Canonical form:

> "To better understand [the pattern], we estimate a [model class] which **allows for, but
> does not impose**, [the mechanism]."

Then explain the mechanism in plain language — no notation whatsoever in the introduction.
The corpus walks a full behavioural ladder (level 0, level 1, level 2, level ∞) in prose.
If your mechanism has a comparable ladder or threshold structure, walk it.

## ¶9 — What the model finds

Estimates and their economic reading. Corroborating outside evidence is powerful here — the
corpus quotes a contemporary trade publication independently reaching the same conclusion.

## ¶10 — Robustness, previewed

*"Our estimates and inferences are robust to…"* — a list, then the model-comparison result.
Comparing your model's fit against a **nested restricted model** (here: the
rational-expectations restriction) is the single most persuasive robustness move for a
structural paper, because it shows the extra structure earns its degrees of freedom.

## ¶11–¶12 — Contribution, one paragraph per claim

Each opens with an explicit performative:

- *"Our paper contributes to the [X] literature by…"*
- *"Our paper provides a new explanation for…"*

Each situates the claim against the prior literature it displaces or complements, with
citation clusters. **This is where related-literature citations live** — there is no
standalone literature review.

Close the second contribution paragraph with a scope disclaimer: *"we do not purport to
estimate the one true model. We rather propose one novel way of looking at the data through
the lens of a workhorse model."*

## ¶13 — "Related to and borrows from several strands"

Three strands, each a sentence or two with a citation cluster: the methodological
literature you borrow from, the adjacent substantive literature, and the tradition your
setting belongs to.

## ¶14 — Generalizability, conceded then defended

The final paragraph. Not a roadmap. Concede the limitation created by your setting's
specificity, then argue the mechanism travels:

> "The flip side of this benefit is the potentially-limited generalizability of our
> findings. We note, however, that the tested theory should generalize to other settings
> and that [the mechanism] appears to matter even in today's world."

## Checklist

- [ ] ¶1 contains no first-person claim about the paper
- [ ] Assumptions enumerated *(a)–(d)* and audited against institutional detail
- [ ] At least one assumption defended with *evidence*, not assertion
- [ ] Exactly one assumption isolated as the contribution
- [ ] Descriptive findings enumerated and stated *before* the model appears
- [ ] Model introduced with "allows for, but does not impose"
- [ ] Zero notation anywhere in the introduction
- [ ] Nested-model comparison mentioned
- [ ] One paragraph per contribution, each with an explicit performative opener
- [ ] Scope disclaimer present
- [ ] Closes on generalizability, not a roadmap
