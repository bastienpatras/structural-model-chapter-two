# What the appendix is missing

Assessment 2026-08-13. Current appendix: **A** Robustness (threshold × σ₁), **B** Figures,
**C** Data and Model Objects, **D** Proofs.

**Diagnosis.** Roughly 29k of the appendix's 34k characters are proofs. This paper's exposure is
empirical, not mathematical: the proofs are the part nobody will contest. The appendix carries
almost none of the defences a referee will actually demand.

Ranked by how much damage the absence does.

---

## 1. Nothing on the ρ̂ clipping ← IMPLEMENTED

40 of 42 partial-pooling apartment markets and 54 of 76 house markets sit at the ρ̂ = 1 boundary;
43 of 76 were outside [0,1] *before* clipping. §4.5 concedes this in prose but no table
characterises it. The only genuinely point-identified observations are the 22 interior house
markets, and a referee will ask what distinguishes them. Everything downstream inherits this
exposure: ĉ₀'s level, the regime classification, and the upper arm of the subsidy distribution.

*Review doc item 7.* Needs: a table comparing boundary / interior / full-unravelling markets on
the structural parameters and on observable market characteristics.

## 2. Nothing on sample selection ← IMPLEMENTED

302 markets enter the estimation sample; 45 apartments and 97 houses come out — attrition of 85%
and 68%. Nothing establishes whether the survivors are representative. Referees ask this
reflexively of any filtered sample, and it is pure description.

*Review doc item 9.* Needs: included versus excluded on price, size, volume, non-disclosure rate,
registry coverage.

## 3. No figure of what K measures over time ← IMPLEMENTED

The registry gap closes from 62.5% to 18.4% of apartment listings and 73.1% to 43.7% of house
listings across the sample, as the post-July-2021 registry fills. This is in §3.2's prose but not
as a figure. It is the cleanest institutional evidence in the paper for what `K` is, and it
pre-empts the obvious attack that the information measure is contaminated.

---

## 4. No per-market estimates table

`results/tables/table_market_estimates.tex` is generated by the pipeline and referenced nowhere in
the paper. Standard for market-level structural work, and it lets a reader verify the clipping and
regime claims independently. Cheap: wire it in and regenerate.

## 5. Nothing on the full-unravelling classification

21 of 97 house markets are labelled full-unravelling on an unfiltered sign test of Δ̂ ≤ 0. Those 21
markets *are* the entire upper arm of the subsidy distribution (verified: the correspondence is
one-for-one, 3/3 apartments and 21/21 houses), so a classification resting on a point-estimate sign
drives the policy result.

*Review doc item 6.* Needs SEs on Δ̂, i.e. bootstrapping the DiD stage. This is the one place a
bootstrap **is** warranted, because Δ̂ is a genuine estimator rather than a deterministic inversion.
Real work.

## 6. Δ̂ has no pre-trend evidence as a gap

`fig:ATT` plots each disclosure group's price path separately (Efficient, Intensive, Missing), not
Δ̂ = Missing − Intensive with confidence bands. The assumptions table concedes common trends is
tested for β₁ but untested for Δ̂ — and Δ̂ is what identifies ρ. Inferable from the three panels but
neither shown nor tested. Feasible from the index panel.

## 7. Nothing on γ₀ = 0, γ₁ = 1

The assumptions table calls this moderate and notes it distorts the *units* of ĉ₀, now the headline
structural number. Transaction data exists and is used for exactly this mapping; a short regression
of transaction on listing price would validate it or give a rescaling factor.
