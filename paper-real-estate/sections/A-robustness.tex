\section{Robustness: The Quality Threshold and the $\sigma_1^*=0$ Restriction}
\label{app:robustness}

Two maintained choices in the baseline specification deserve separate scrutiny, because each
is an assumption rather than an estimate.

The first is where the binary quality space is cut. The baseline places the boundary between
D and E, so that $\theta=1$ corresponds to grades A--D. This follows the rental-ban
discontinuity of \emph{loi Climat et R\'esilience} discussed in
Section~\ref{ss:institutions}, and it is the cut for which the price-index contrast and the
listing-level quality measure refer to the same partition of grades. The natural alternative
places the boundary between C and D.

The second is the treatment of informed sellers who withhold. Because such a listing displays
no score, its quality cannot be read from the listing itself. The baseline assigns
$\theta=0$ by revealed preference, invoking Corollary~\ref{cor:att_hq}. That assignment
is an assumption. It makes
$\hat\sigma_{1,m}$ identically zero by construction, so the corollary cannot be assessed
against the baseline estimates. The alternative reads the withheld grade from the ADEME
registry, which records it for every matched listing, leaving $\hat\sigma_{1,m}$ measured. Under limited attention (Section~\ref{ss:attention}), high-quality hiding is possible only if silence outprices a verified efficient rating and the market is in Complete Adverse Selection (Proposition~\ref{prop:att_seller_types}), so the registry reading, which leaves $\hat\sigma_1$ measured, is the specification that does not rule it out.

Three results follow; below, ``benchmark cost'' means the full-attention cost $\hat c_{0,m}^{\mathrm{FA}}=\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m$ that the table reports. The paper's headline cost under limited attention is the predicted premium, whose mean is $\hat\Delta$ whatever $\hat e^*_m$ is; it moves with the threshold only through the estimation sample.

First, the anchored estimate of effective attention to ratings does not depend on the $\sigma_1$ assignment, and the
benchmark cost only mildly so. Holding the threshold fixed, relaxing the restriction leaves
$\hat\rho_m$ completely unchanged in both segments: the anchored estimator
$\hat\rho_m=\hat\beta_{1,m}/\hat\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:anchored_identification})
never involves $\sigma_1$ at all, so there is nothing for the assignment to move. $\hat
c^{\mathrm{FA}}_{0,m}$ moves modestly, from 2.41\% to 2.62\% for apartments and from 3.45\% to 3.56\% for
houses, through its dependence on $\hat e^*_m$, which does respond to the assignment. The
benchmark cost is therefore not an artefact of the revealed-preference assignment, even
though $\hat\sigma_{0,m}$ itself is quite sensitive to it: imposing $\theta=0$ places every
informed non-discloser in the low-quality group and inflates the measured hiding rate by
roughly a factor of two and a half (15.2\% against 5.9\% for apartments, 6.8\% against 2.8\%
for houses).

Second, the restriction $\sigma_1^*=0$ is not satisfied exactly, but the model's predicted
\emph{ordering} holds under the maintained threshold. With quality read from the registry and
the cut at the rental-ban boundary, $\hat\sigma_{1,m} = 5.1\%$ against
$\hat\sigma_{0,m} = 5.9\%$ for apartments, and $2.1\%$ against $2.8\%$ for houses. Informed
sellers of low-quality properties withhold more often than informed sellers of high-quality
ones, as the model requires, though the gap is narrow. Under the C--D cut the ordering
reverses for apartments ($6.5\%$ against $5.1\%$) and is effectively a tie for houses ($2.5\%$
against $2.5\%$, indistinguishable at this precision). The threshold that the institutional
setting selects is thus also the one under which the behavioural restriction survives, which
we read as support for the baseline cut rather than as independent confirmation of the
corollary.

Third, the threshold matters a great deal for the level of the benchmark cost, and this is
the most consequential specification choice for the benchmark cost. Moving the cut from C--D to D--E raises
$\hat c^{\mathrm{FA}}_{0,m}$ from roughly 1.1--1.8\% of property value to 2.4--3.6\%, a little more than a
doubling in both segments. The mechanism is transparent. With A--D counted as high quality,
the average quality of the non-disclosing pool $\hat e^*_m$ rises substantially (the informed
high-quality share $\hat{\bar\theta}_m$ alone more than doubles, from roughly 27--29\% under
the C--D cut to 60--66\% under D--E), and $c^{\mathrm{FA}}_{0,m}=\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:what_identified}) scales with it. Readers who prefer the narrower
definition of ``high quality'' should therefore read the benchmark cost as the smaller
figure throughout; the relative ordering of the benchmark cost across apartments and houses
is unaffected by the threshold choice.

\section{Identification of $\rho_m$: A Formal Treatment}
\label{app:rho_identification}

The naive market-by-market ratio $\hat r_m=\hat\Delta_m/(\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m)$ is
degenerate: it returns approximately $1$ regardless of the true buyer-sensitivity share, because
$\rho_m$ cancels between numerator and denominator under the model's own pricing equations. This
appendix states the identification failure formally, and the external anchor that resolves it.

The statements below are written for the full-attention benchmark. Under limited attention (Section~\ref{ss:attention}), the scale non-identification result (Proposition~\ref{prop:scale_nonid}) and the anchored estimate of $\rho_m$ hold with $\rho_m$ replaced by $\rho_m\omega_1$, the effective attention to a displayed rating, for given $\psi$ and $e^{\rm def}$: prices depend on $\rho_m$ and $\omega_1$ only through $b_m=\rho_m\omega_1\beta_1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:att_prices}), and a silent listing sells at $b_m\tilde e$ in place of $b_m e$. The other statements are benchmark-only: the identification and bounds of the concealment cost (Proposition~\ref{prop:what_identified}(ii)--(iv)), Lemma~\ref{lem:model_bounds}(i), and the $c_0$ half of Proposition~\ref{prop:anchored_identification}, and the specification test of Corollary~\ref{cor:ratio_specification_test}, whose probability limit is $\tilde e/e^*$ and not one when $\psi<1$ (it is one if $\zeta^{\rm def}=\bar \zeta^{\rm sil}$). Under limited attention the concealment cost is read off the premium, $c_{0,m}=\Delta_m$ in Partial Pooling (Proposition~\ref{prop:att_identification}(iv)).

\subsection*{The scale non-identification result}

Write $b_m\equiv\rho_m\beta_1$ for the composite parameter that governs every observable price
implication of the model.

\begin{proposition}[Scale non-identification of buyer sensitivity]
\label{prop:scale_nonid}
Under the Linear Pricing Assumption~\ref{assum:linear_pricing}, fix any
parameter vector with $\rho_m\in(0,1]$ and $\beta_1>0$ for all markets $m$, and any
$\lambda\in(0,(\max_m\rho_m)^{-1}]$. The reparametrization $(\rho_m,\beta_1)\mapsto
(\lambda\rho_m,\beta_1/\lambda)$ is admissible and induces an identical joint distribution of
observed prices, an identical equilibrium hiding rate $\sigma_0^*$, and an identical
equilibrium regime, in every market and every period. Consequently $\{\rho_m\}$ and $\beta_1$
are not separately identified by any price-based moment implied by the model, for any number of
markets $M$; only the composites $\{b_m\}$ and $\{c_{0,m}\}$ are.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every observable implication of the model is a function of $(\rho_m,\beta_1)$ through the
product $b_m$ alone. For a disclosed listing, $p^*(d=1,\theta)=p_{\rm L}+\rho_m\beta_1\theta
=p_{\rm L}+b_m\theta$, where $p_{\rm L}$ is the price of a displayed inefficient rating, which the reparametrization leaves unchanged because the free level $\bar V$ absorbs the change. For a non-disclosed listing, $p^*(d=0)=p_{\rm L}+\rho_m\beta_1
e=p_{\rm L}+b_m e$. The hiding incentive is $\Delta(\sigma_0)=\rho_m\beta_1 e(\sigma_0)-c_{0,m}
=b_m e(\sigma_0)-c_{0,m}$, so the equilibrium $\sigma_0^*$ solving $\Delta(\sigma_0^*)=0$
depends on $(\rho_m,\beta_1)$ only through $b_m$. The regime boundaries of
Proposition~\ref{prop:equilibrium} ($c_{0,m}$ against $b_m\bar\theta_m$ and $b_m e(1)$) involve
$(\rho_m,\beta_1)$ the same way. Since $b_m=\rho_m\beta_1=(\lambda\rho_m)(\beta_1/\lambda)$ is
invariant under the stated map and $c_{0,m}$ is untouched, the reparametrized vector reproduces
every observable channel exactly. Admissibility requires $\lambda\rho_m\le1$ for all $m$, i.e.\
$\lambda\le(\max_m\rho_m)^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The raw ratio is a specification-test statistic, not an estimator]
\label{cor:ratio_specification_test}
$\hat r_m=\hat\Delta_m/(\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m)$ satisfies $\mathrm{plim}\,\hat r_m=1$
at full attention ($\psi=\omega_1=1$) and under Assumption~\ref{assum:no_pre_pricing}, for every true $\rho_m\in(0,1]$, since under the model $\hat\Delta_m=b_m e^*_m$ and
$\hat\beta_{1,m}=b_m$ share the same composite $b_m$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale_nonid}. It is the sample analogue of the over-identifying
restriction $\Delta_m=\beta_{1,m}\,e^*_m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:model_bounds} below), not an
estimate of buyer sensitivity; its deviation from unity is a specification-test statistic, and
clipping it to $[0,1]$ suppresses that test rather than estimating anything.
\end{corollary}

\subsection*{What is identified without an external anchor}

\begin{proposition}[Identification of the composite and of $c_0$]
\label{prop:what_identified}
Given consistent estimates of $(\pi_m,\bar\theta_m,\sigma_{0,m})$ from the ADEME registry match
and $\hat\beta_{1,m}$ from the disclosed-price DiD:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $b_m$ is point identified: $b_m=\beta_{1,m}$.
    \item If $\sigma_{0,m}\in(0,1)$ (Partial Pooling), $c_{0,m}$ is point identified,
        \emph{without reference to $\rho_m$ or $\beta_1$ separately}:
        $c_{0,m}=\beta_{1,m}\cdot e(\sigma_{0,m};\pi_m,\bar\theta_m)$.
    \item If $\sigma_{0,m}=0$ (Cost-Deterred Hiding), $c_{0,m}\ge\beta_{1,m}\bar\theta_m$; if
        $\sigma_{0,m}=1$ (Complete Adverse Selection), $c_{0,m}\le\beta_{1,m}\,e(1)$.
    \item $\Delta_m$ is an over-identifying restriction: $\Delta_m=\beta_{1,m}\,e(\sigma_{0,m})$,
        i.e.\ $\Delta_m=c_{0,m}$ in Partial Pooling.
    \item $\rho_m$ itself is not identified (Proposition~\ref{prop:scale_nonid}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is the disclosed-price equation $\beta_{1,m}=p^*(d=1,\theta=1)-p^*(d=1,\theta=0)=b_m$.
(ii): in Partial Pooling the indifference condition
$\Delta(e(\sigma_0^*))=c_{0,m}$ reads $b_m\,e(\sigma_{0,m})=c_{0,m}$; substitute $b_m=\beta_{1,m}$
from (i). (iii) follows from the regime boundaries of Proposition~\ref{prop:equilibrium}
evaluated at $b_m=\beta_{1,m}$. (iv) combines (i) with the non-disclosed pricing equation
$\Delta_m\equiv p^*(d=0)-p^*(d=1,\theta=0)=b_m\,e(\sigma_{0,m})$. (v) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:scale_nonid}.
\end{proof}

This result has an immediate practical consequence: $\hat c_{0,m}=\hat\beta_{1,m}\cdot\hat
e^*_m(\hat\sigma_{0,m})$ is the full-attention estimator of the concealment cost (Appendix~\ref{app:c0_fullattention}), for both property types, and it does not require $\rho_m$ to
be identified at all. Under limited attention Section~\ref{sec:identification} reads $c_{0,m}$ off the premium in Partial Pooling and estimates it as the predicted premium.

\begin{lemma}[Model-implied bounds on the non-disclosure premium]
\label{lem:model_bounds}
(i) $0\le\Delta_m/\beta_{1,m}=e_m\le\bar\theta_m\le1$, since the belief function is bounded
above by $\bar\theta_m$ (attained at $\sigma_0=0$). (ii) If the market is in Partial Pooling both before and after the July~2021 reform and $c_{0,m}$ is
invariant to it (the identifying assumption of
Section~\ref{sec:identification}), the model predicts a DiD non-disclosure premium of zero:
since $\Delta_{m,t}=c_{0,m}$ in Partial Pooling at every date $t$, its post-minus-pre change is
zero whenever $c_{0,m}$ does not move at the reform.
\end{lemma}

\noindent Under Assumption~\ref{assum:no_pre_pricing} buyers price no energy before the reform, the pre-reform market is not in Partial Pooling, and part~(ii) does not apply: the DiD premium then measures the post-reform premium, which is how Section~\ref{sec:identification} reads it.

\subsection*{Point identification under an external anchor}

\begin{assumption}[Energy-cost anchor]
\label{assum:energy_anchor}
For each market $m$, an external, non-price estimate of the sensitive buyer's willingness to
pay for verified quality is available as the capitalised energy-cost saving:
\begin{equation}
    \beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}} = \kappa\cdot\frac{A_m}{P_m}, \qquad
    A_m = \big(\bar E_{0,m}-\bar E_{1,m}\big)\cdot p^E\cdot a(r,T), \qquad
    a(r,T)=\frac{1-(1+r)^{-T}}{r},
    \label{eq:energy_anchor}
\end{equation}
where $\bar E_{\theta,m}$ is mean certified primary-energy consumption (kWh$_{\mathrm{ep}}$/m$^2$/yr)
of quality-$\theta$ dwellings among ADEME-matched ($K=1$) listings in market $m$ (pooling
\emph{both} disclosed and strategically-hidden listings, since restricting to disclosed-only
listings would reintroduce selection on the seller's own hiding decision), $p^E$ is a
blended primary-energy-equivalent price, $P_m$ is the local price level, and $a(r,T)$ discounts
the annual saving over a horizon $T$ at rate $r$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Point identification of $\rho_m$ under Assumption~\ref{assum:energy_anchor}]
\label{prop:anchored_identification}
Under Assumption~\ref{assum:energy_anchor} with $\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}>0$ known and $\omega_1=1$ (otherwise the first equality identifies $\rho_m\omega_1$), and, for the second equality, at $\psi=1$ and in Partial Pooling,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_m=\frac{\beta_{1,m}}{\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}}, \qquad
    c_{0,m}=\beta_{1,m}\cdot e(\sigma_{0,m};\pi_m,\bar\theta_m)
    \label{eq:anchored_estimator}
\end{equation}
are point identified. The model's constraint $\rho_m\le1$ becomes the testable inequality
$\beta_{1,m}\le\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}$, rather than an imposed clip.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Jacobian of $(\beta_{1,m},\sigma_{0,m})$ with respect to $(\rho_m,c_{0,m})$ at a Partial
Pooling equilibrium, holding $\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}$ fixed, is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}} & 0 \\[2pt]
k\,\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}} & -k\,\rho_m\,\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}}/c_{0,m}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
k\equiv\frac{(1-\pi_m)\bar\theta_m}{c_{0,m}\,\pi_m(1-\bar\theta_m)},
\]
with determinant $-k\,\rho_m\,(\beta_{1,m}^{\mathrm{anchor}})^2/c_{0,m}\ne0$ whenever
$\rho_m,c_{0,m}>0$ and $\pi_m,\bar\theta_m\in(0,1)$. Local identification follows; global
identification follows from strict monotonicity of $\beta_{1,m}\mapsto\rho_m$ in
\eqref{eq:anchored_estimator} and, given $\rho_m$, of $\sigma_{0,m}\mapsto c_{0,m}$ through the
strictly decreasing belief function of Lemma~\ref{lem:belief_monotone}.
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Diagnostics reported alongside the anchored estimates
(Table~\ref{tab:did_results} and Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).} At full attention, the measured wedge
$\hat\lambda_m\equiv\hat\Delta_m-\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:model_bounds}/Corollary~\ref{cor:ratio_specification_test}) is kept at the market level
(its mean is in Table~\ref{tab:attention}) rather than absorbed into $\hat c_{0,m}$; the concentration statistic
$\bar\Lambda'=M^{-1}\sum_m\hat\rho_m(1-\hat\rho_m)$, restricted to markets with
$\hat\rho_m\in[0,1]$, gates whether the anchored $\hat\rho_m$ sample carries enough interior
variation to support a covariate index rather than only a common estimate; and the count of
markets violating $\hat\rho_m\le1$ is reported directly rather than clipped away.

\subsection*{Supply-Side Stability across the Reform}
\label{app:supply_stability}

The reform operated on the demand side, and the identification argument of Section~\ref{sec:identification} treats the supply side as stable across July 2021. Two remarks bear on that assumption, an alternative design that fails and the timing of the two gaps.

First, an alternative design based on a Bartik instrument, interacting wholesale gas price variation with the pre-reform share of low-efficiency properties in each market, fails at the first stage ($F$-statistic: 1.0 to 1.6), so it cannot corroborate the assumption. The French \emph{bouclier tarifaire} (energy price shield, October 2021 to 2023) decoupled household energy costs from the TTF gas price spike that followed the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and so removed the demand-side variation the instrument was designed to exploit.

Second, the timing of the two gaps is hard to reconcile with a supply-side shift. These two gaps measure distinct economic objects: $\beta_{1,m,t}$ captures buyers' willingness to pay for verified high quality, while $\Delta_{m,t}$ reflects the informational wedge created by strategic non-disclosure. They respond to different primitives of the model. A supply-side confound (a shift in $\pi_m$, $\bar\theta_m$, or seller composition at the reform date) would generically move these two gaps by different amounts and in potentially different directions, since the mechanisms through which supply composition enters each gap differ. The fact that both gaps are jointly flat before July 2021 and both shift upward simultaneously after the reform, at the same markets and on the same calendar date, is difficult to reconcile with a supply-side story. It is exactly what the demand-side interpretation predicts: legal opposability increases the return to EPC quality for sensitive buyers, raising both the price of verified high quality ($\beta_{1,m,t}$) and the information premium extracted by non-disclosers ($\Delta_{m,t}$) at the same moment. Because the disclosure obligation and its associated liability predate the reform (loi ALUR, Article L271-4 CCH), we treat $c_{0,m}$ as unchanged across July 2021. We do not sign the bias a violation of this assumption would introduce into the estimates, since the anchor is constructed independently of the reform-induced price changes and the direction would depend on exactly how a supply-side shift propagates into the disclosed-price gap $\hat\beta_{1,m}$.

\subsection*{The Naive Ratio in the Data}
\label{app:naive_ratio}

With $\hat\beta_{1,m}$, $\hat\Delta_m$ and $\hat e^*_m$ all in hand, the premium equation $\Delta_m=\rho_m\beta_{1,m}e^*_m$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:premium_derived}) appears to deliver buyer sensitivity by rearrangement, as the ratio $\hat\Delta_m/(\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m)$, formed either in levels or in reform-induced changes. It does not, and the reason is internal to the model rather than a shortcoming of the data. Buyers who price the certificate and buyers who ignore it are aggregated into a single market price, so the sensitive-buyer share never appears on its own. It appears multiplied by the physical size of the quality difference, and only ever in that combination. The disclosed-price gap equals that product; the gap between silent and intensive listings equals the same product scaled by the pool belief. Their ratio is therefore the pool belief itself, which is exactly what the construction then divides by, so at full attention it returns approximately one whatever the true $\rho_m$ happens to be. Under limited attention its probability limit is $\tilde e/e^*$ and not one, so a ratio above one is the model's prediction when $\psi<1$ and $e^{\rm def}>e^*$. Pooling markets buys nothing, since every market contributes the same degenerate identity. What the ratio does measure is whether the full-attention restriction $\Delta_m=\beta_{1,m}e^*_m$ holds in the data, which makes it a specification statistic rather than an estimator. The results of this appendix state and prove this formally, and Figure~\ref{fig:rho_saturation} shows what it looks like in the data: apartment markets sit almost entirely above the line where $\hat\Delta_m$ would exceed $\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m$, which is the region the model predicts when $\psi_\delta<1$, in nearly every market and not in a handful of outliers.

\subsection*{The Full-Attention Estimate of the Hiding Cost}
\label{app:c0_fullattention}

At full attention ($\psi=1$) the concealment cost is the disclosed-price gap scaled by the belief prevailing at the observed hiding rate, and this full-attention cost, $\hat c_{0,m}^{\rm FA}=\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m(\hat\sigma_{0,m})$, uses neither the anchor nor $\rho_m$. It is the equilibrium indifference condition read backwards: in partial pooling the marginal informed hider is exactly indifferent between disclosing and staying silent, so the revenue she gives up by remaining in the pool must be what silence costs her. One free parameter is matched to one observed moment, the hiding rate among informed low-quality sellers, and the match is exact. Appendix~\ref{app:rho_identification} sets out both arguments (Propositions~\ref{prop:what_identified} and~\ref{prop:anchored_identification}). Under limited attention the premium equals the hiding cost in Partial Pooling (Proposition~\ref{prop:att_identification}(iv)), and the paper calibrates the cost to the predicted premium, whose mean is $\hat\Delta$. The two estimates coincide at $\psi=1$ and differ on average by the wedge $\hat\lambda_m\equiv\hat\Delta_m-\hat\beta_{1,m}\hat e^*_m$, which is therefore the quantity that identifies attention to silence. The mean wedge is \attLamApt{} log points for apartments ($t=\attLamTApt$) and \attLamHouse{} for houses ($t=\attLamTHouse$), so the full-attention restriction is rejected in apartments and not in houses.

The dispersion of the anchored estimate across markets is reported here. The anchor gives a point estimate of $\rho_m\omega_1$ in each market rather than
a value pressed against a boundary. The anchored estimate of effective attention to displayed
ratings averages $\attRhoMeanApt{}\%$ for apartments and $\attRhoMeanHouse{}\%$ for houses, and markets
spread around those averages in a way the estimator does not manufacture, with a cross-market
standard deviation of $\attRhoSdApt{}$ and $\attRhoSdHouse{}$ percentage points respectively. A ratio-based construction returns nothing
of the kind; it piles the whole distribution onto the upper bound. That bound is now something
the data can refuse. Since effective attention to displayed ratings cannot exceed one, the anchored estimate requires
the disclosed-price gap to stay below the energy-bill benchmark, and the requirement fails in
only \attNRhoGtOneApt{} of \attNMarketsApt{} apartment markets and \attNRhoGtOneHouse{} of \attNMarketsHouse{} house markets. Almost everywhere, the running-cost
saving an efficient dwelling delivers is large enough on its own terms to account for the price
advantage certified efficiency commands, without supposing that every buyer is paying attention.

One consequence of the closed-form estimator is worth stating plainly, because it closes off a
finding the model might otherwise appear to offer. The concealment cost is the disclosed-price
gap scaled by a belief that is itself confined, by construction, between the value it would take
if every informed low-quality seller hid and the value it would take if none did. The cost can
therefore never land outside the Partial Pooling band, and every market in the sample is

Under limited attention, setting $\hat c_{0,m}=\hat\Delta_m$ market by market, the classification differs: \attRawNCdApt{} apartment markets are in Cost-Deterred Hiding, \attRawNCasApt{} in Complete Adverse Selection and \attRawNPpApt{} in Partial Pooling, against \attRawNCdHouse{}, \attRawNCasHouse{} and \attRawNPpHouse{} house markets in the three regimes.
